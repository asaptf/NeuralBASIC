# Code of Conduct

## In short

Be decent to people. This is a project about learning, and learning means being wrong in public —
so the way we treat someone who has misunderstood something matters more here than it does in most
repositories.

## Expected behaviour

- Assume the person asking a basic question is smart and new, not slow. That is the whole premise of
  the app; it should also be how the repository works.
- Critique the code, the claim, or the pedagogy — not the person.
- When you correct someone, say what is actually true and why. "That's wrong" without the *why*
  teaches nothing.
- Accept correction the same way. If a measurement contradicts you, the measurement wins.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks; demeaning or discriminatory comments.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions or reviews.
- Condescension aimed at people who are learning — including sneering at "obvious" questions.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, code reviews, commit messages, and any other project space,
as well as when someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behaviour to the maintainer privately. Use GitHub's
[private vulnerability and abuse reporting](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
on this repository, or contact the maintainer through their GitHub profile at
[@asaptf](https://github.com/asaptf).

Reports will be handled confidentially. The maintainer will respond as promptly as they reasonably
can, and may remove comments, close contributions, or block accounts as needed.

Because this project currently has a single maintainer, there is no separate committee to appeal to.
If your report concerns the maintainer themselves, GitHub's abuse reporting above goes to GitHub
rather than to this project, and that is the right channel to use.

## Enforcement

Consequences scale with severity and repetition: a private word, a public correction, removal of a
comment or contribution, or a ban. There is no formula, and no obligation to warn first where the
behaviour is severe.
